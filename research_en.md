# Who Is Burning Down Russia, Digitally and Physically. A Research Paper

## Why I Am Writing This

I am not a journalist. I am not an analyst from some consulting firm. I am someone who, over the past few years, started noticing that events the media presents as unrelated episodes actually fit together into one picture. And that picture looks like someone is methodically destroying Russia from two directions at once: the physical and the digital.

I will not beat around the bush. Let's go.

## Part 1. Crypto Exchanges: Blocking Russian Accounts as the Opening Salvo of Financial War

### What Happened

Garantex. March 2025. The US Secret Service, working with German and Finnish authorities, seized the exchange's domain and froze over $26 million in assets. Indictments were unsealed against key figures including Aleksandr Mira Serda and Aleksej Besciokov. Besciokov was arrested in India. Not in Russia, not in Ukraine, in India. That tells you something about the global reach of this operation.

Binance sold its Russian business to CommEX and effectively left the market. Direct fiat deposits through Russian banks were discontinued. Coinbase fully blocked Russians. Try registering with a Russian IP, you simply cannot get in. Coinbase froze 25,000 Russian accounts.

And what replaced all this? Garantex did not die. It morphed into a decentralized network operating through the UAE, Kyrgyzstan, and Georgia. Sergey Mendeleev, the man behind Garantex, launched a service called Exved that continues processing financial flows. Grinex appeared, registered in December 2024 with an interface nearly identical to Garantex. Through the A7A5 stablecoin, users migrated their balances from the old exchange to the new one.

The EU responded with 20 sanctions packages. The 20th package, April 2026, imposed a blanket ban on all transactions with any crypto provider registered in Russia or Belarus. Not individual exchanges, the entire Russian crypto services sector. Plus a ban on the digital ruble and RUBx. The EU document explicitly states: the digital ruble was "purpose-built for sanctions evasion."

### What This Means

Russia is being cut off from the global financial system not piece by piece, but wholesale. SWIFT disconnected. Crypto exchanges blocked. Stablecoins frozen. Tether blocked Garantex's wallets holding over 2.5 billion rubles. What is left? A domestic system. The digital ruble. And total control.

## Part 2. Destroying Data Centers: War Against Servers

### What Happened

From October 8 to October 11, 2026, Ukrainian drones attacked three of Yandex's six data centers. Three out of six. In four days.

October 8. Sasovo, Ryazan Oblast. Fire. The data center was completely shut down. It housed two of Yandex's three supercomputers: Chervonenkis and Lyapunov. Satellite imagery shows one wing of the building burned to the ground.

October 9. Kaluga. The largest and most modern data center, launched in late 2023, with 63 MW of capacity and 3,800 server racks. Several modules completely destroyed.

October 11. Vladimir. The third targeted facility. Capacity of 40-50 MW, 2,880 racks. The Galushkin supercomputer was destroyed by fire.

After this, more than 30 Yandex services stopped working. Taxi, food delivery, Yandex Pay (users could not withdraw money from their accounts), Kinopoisk, Yandex Music, Alice smart assistant, Smart Home. Services in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan also experienced outages.

Yandex had run drills for taking one data center offline and redistributing the load. But three were hit. Attempts to migrate to VK Cloud, Selectel, and K2Cloud failed because there were no free server racks available in the country.

Experts estimate restoration will take anywhere from six months to 2-2.5 years. The equipment is under sanctions, making it extremely difficult and expensive to procure. Damages could reach 30 billion rubles. Some of the most powerful supercomputers in Russia were destroyed, and the country only has about ten in total.

### Context

This was not an isolated event. From September 23 to 28, Russian forces damaged or destroyed at least six data centers in Ukraine. Eight Ukrainian companies lost their data centers. Zelensky called the strikes on Yandex a mirror response. Russia's Ministry of Defense described the strikes on Ukrainian data centers as "a digital detox in forced mode."

### Why Destroy Data Centers

This is where it gets interesting. The Ukrainian side says it is a response to strikes on their infrastructure. And that is true. But let us look at the bigger picture.

Data centers are not just servers. They are the backbone of the digital economy. Cloud platforms, banking systems, payment services, government registries, marketplaces, logistics. When you knock out data centers, you are not just breaking Yandex. You are creating the conditions for a collapse of the entire digital infrastructure. And in a context where Russia is actively transitioning to the digital ruble, where online cash registers send data on every transaction to the Federal Tax Service via fiscal data operators in real time, where government services and banking systems depend on the cloud, destroying data centers is a strike on the nervous system of the country.

## Part 3. Oil Refineries: Burning Down the Fuel Infrastructure

### The Numbers

In 2024, Ukrainian drones attacked Russian oil refineries and fuel depots at least 81 times. In the first seven months of 2025, 22 refineries were hit, affecting facilities with a combined processing capacity of 180 million tons of oil per year. That is more than half of the country's total capacity.

According to Reuters, by August 2025 roughly 23% of Russia's refining capacity was offline. Of that, 17% was shut down specifically due to drone strikes. In September 2025, the agency reported that drone attacks had temporarily knocked out up to 21% of capacity. That is about 1.4 million barrels of oil per day.

By June 2026, according to the International Energy Agency, Russian oil refining had fallen to 3.8 million barrels per day. This is the lowest level in 20 years, roughly 30% below the previous year. Gasoline production dropped 20%, diesel production fell 30%.

Of Russia's 10 largest refineries, Ukrainian drones struck 8.

### Specifics

Ryazan Refinery. The largest fuel supplier to Moscow and the surrounding region. Processed 13.1 million tons in 2024. That is 5% of Russia's total refining capacity. Hit nine times in 2025. Attacked three times in the month before it was shut down in November.

Volgograd Refinery (Lukoil). Fully shut down in February 2025, August 2025, and attacked again in 2026.

Tuapse Refinery (Rosneft). Stopped operations after attacks in May, July, and October 2024, then again in April and May 2026.

Moscow Refinery. Hit in September 2024, May 2026, and then struck by two waves of drones on June 16-18, 2026. According to Reuters, it is unlikely to resume operations before the end of 2026.

Omsk Refinery, the largest in Russia, in Siberia, 1,800 miles from Kyiv. Hit in July 2026.

Drones reached refineries in Angarsk (Irkutsk Oblast, 3,000 miles from Ukraine), Achinsk (Krasnoyarsk Krai), and Salavat (Bashkortostan, 1,400 km from the border).

### What This Produced

Russia, the world's third-largest oil exporter, began importing gasoline. From India, Belarus, and Kazakhstan. A ban on gasoline exports has been in place since February 2025, extended through January 2027. Exports of diesel and aviation fuel were also restricted.

Crimea declared a state of emergency because of gasoline shortages. In Krasnodar, one-third of gas stations shut down. In Irkutsk, people waited in line for 18 hours. A woman with a baby waited 18 hours to fill up. A mother and child. In Russia, an oil-producing country.

## Part 4. The Looting of Gas Stations: Corruption as a Tool of Collapse

### What Happened

Since the beginning of 2026, 322 gas stations closed for "renovation or rebranding." That is 70% more than the same period last year. Plus another 214 stations have been down since last year. In total, more than 500 gas stations are not operating, nearly 2% of all stations in the country.

150 gas stations have been put up for sale. Prices range from 1 to 150 million rubles. In Ufa, a network of 13 stations is being sold for 350 million. Gazprom is selling gas stations in the Astrakhan, Rostov, Tambov, and Nizhny Novgorod regions. Lukoil has listed stations in the Tver, Tyumen, Chelyabinsk, Kaluga regions and Perm Krai.

Of Russia's 27,700 operating gas stations, roughly 19,000 are privately owned. They have no refining capacity of their own. They buy fuel on the exchange or from refineries. And the major oil companies supply their own stations first. Then the large regional chains. And the independents get whatever is left. If anything is left.

### The Scheme

This, in my view, is the most repulsive part of the whole story. The Federal Antimonopoly Service (FAS) opened 58 cases in the fuel market. It turned out that three companies had been coordinating to buy up all the fuel on the exchange. As a result, honest market participants, independent gas stations, farmers, and transport companies, could not buy fuel at market prices. They were forced to buy from the cartel at a massive markup.

Starting July 21, 2026, the St. Petersburg Exchange introduced a mechanism where gasoline could only be purchased for end-consumer use. This means traders cannot resell fuel, exchange liquidity drops, and fuel becomes even less accessible for independent gas stations.

Who wins? The major oil companies. They buy up bankrupt private gas stations for pennies. The fuel crisis is not a side effect. It is a tool for market redistribution.

### Illegal Gas Stations

A separate story. According to experts, Russia has between 1,000 and 1,500 illegal gas stations. In the Saratov Oblast, about 30 out of 300 stations are illegal. In some regions, there are more illegal stations than legal ones. They mimic the branding of major companies, deceive customers, operate without permits, without oversight. And nothing happens because the moratorium on inspections has been extended to 2030.

## Part 5. The Digital Ruble: The Final Chord

### Timeline

July 24, 2023. Putin signed the law introducing the digital ruble.

August 15, 2023. Pilot launch. 600 people, 22 companies.

By December 2025. 20 banks, 2,500 users, 90,000 transactions.

January 1, 2026. The digital ruble began being used for government payments: salaries for officials, social benefits, maintenance of government property.

September 1, 2026. Mandatory launch. All 12 systemically important banks and retail companies with revenue above 120 million rubles are required to accept the digital ruble. In the first month, 220,000 accounts were opened, 3.7 times the Central Bank's forecast.

September 1, 2027. All banks with universal licenses and companies with revenue above 30 million rubles.

September 1, 2028. Remaining banks and companies with revenue above 5 million rubles.

### What This Means in Practice

The digital ruble is not just another payment method. It is a direct transaction between a citizen's wallet and the Central Bank's platform. Banks serve as intermediaries, but the digital ruble itself is stored on the regulator's platform. Every transaction is visible. Every ruble is tracked. Analyst Alexei Kritchevsky says it plainly: "This is a tool of control: how funds are spent, where they come from, what money people live on."

The digital ruble has programmable smart contracts and AML monitoring built in at the base layer. This means the Central Bank gets the technical ability to set spending conditions for individual monetary units. Imagine: the government can decide what you are allowed to spend "your" money on. Not just block your card, but literally program where each ruble goes.

Professor Katasonov calls this an "electronic concentration camp." If you behave incorrectly, they simply freeze your account with digital rubles.

### What the Central Bank Says

Cash will not be abolished. Use is voluntary. Bank secrecy is protected. No forced conversion.

But here are the facts. Cash is being gradually phased out. You cannot pay with cash on Moscow's public transit. Banks are reducing the number of ATMs. And starting in 2025, amendments took effect requiring markets and fairs to use online cash registers and transmit payment data directly through the SBP/NSPK system. Every transaction through a fiscal data operator goes to the Federal Tax Service in real time.

Plus, the EU has already sanctioned the digital ruble. The 20th sanctions package. A transaction ban effective May 24, 2026. The EU calls the digital ruble "purpose-built for sanctions evasion."

5. The digital ruble is being forced into existence. Every transaction visible to the Central Bank. Programmable money. Smart contracts with built-in AML.
